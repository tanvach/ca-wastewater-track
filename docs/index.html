How is this calculated?
  1. Raw Concentration: We use the raw viral concentration (copies/L of wastewater or copies/g of sludge, depending on the lab). Ideally, we would normalize by PMMoV (a harmless fecal plant virus) to account for dilution, but PMMoV data is unavailable in this specific dataset.
  2. One Series per Line: Some plants are tested by more than one lab (in units that can't be compared), at more than one site, or with more than one assay. Each line uses a single series: one that is still reporting, preferring samples taken at the plant over upstream sites, and among those the one sampled on the most days over the last 18 months (near-equal counts go to the longer history).
  3. Non-Detects Count: Samples where the virus wasn't found count as 0, so a rare detection shows as a brief bump, not a trend. Viruses with no detections in the selected range aren't drawn.
  4. Robust Smoothing: We use a two-step filter (Median then Rolling Average) to remove single-day outliers and show the true trend. The median compares detections only with other detections, so a lone detection among non-detects is kept (it can't be told apart from a one-off error).
  5. % of Peak: levels are shown as a percentage of the highest smoothed value observed in the last 18 months.
(Note: CDPH updates data once daily.)
